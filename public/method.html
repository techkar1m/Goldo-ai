The method

Every rule, written out.

Goldo is a frame-based expert system with two tiers of production rules. Nothing below is hidden behind a model — this page is the actual matching logic, in the order it runs.

Knowledge representation

Investors are frames, not prose

Every investor in the knowledge base is stored as a frame with fixed slots. Storing them this way — rather than as free text — is what makes rule-based matching possible at all: every rule reads a named slot, and every slot uses the same controlled vocabulary the classifier labels your description with.

Multi-valued slots are semicolon-separated. A fund that does both pre-seed and seed stores pre-seed; seed, and the rules treat each entry independently — taking the best fit across them, never the average.

Tier 1 · eliminator rules

Hard filters: two ways to be ruled out

Eliminators are absolute — a firm that fails one never reaches the scorer. Both rules require positive evidence of a mismatch, so an investor with a blank stage or blank geography always survives to be scored.

E1 Stage mismatch eliminate 
IF   startup.stage is known
AND  investor.stage_focus is not empty
AND  min( |ladder(startup.stage) − ladder(each investor stage)| ) > 1
THEN drop the investor
     reason: "stage mismatch (invests seed/series-a)"

Stages sit on a seven-rung ladder. The rule measures the distance from your stage to the investor's nearest stated stage, and drops them only if even their closest stage is more than one rung away. A buyout fund is therefore ruled out for a pre-seed startup, while a seed fund is not.

E2 Geography mismatch eliminate 
IF   startup.geography is known
AND  investor.geography is not empty
AND  no investor region appears in covers(startup.geography)
THEN drop the investor

A market is covered either by name or by a region that contains it. Both supported markets accept the same wider set, so a fund that only writes “Southeast Asia” still competes for a Malaysian founder.

Tier 2 · scorer rules

Weighted scoring: five ways to earn points

Survivors start at zero and accumulate points. Stage and geography lead the weights because those slots are reliably populated across the knowledge base; sector is worth just as much when it's present, but most firms in this market never published a thesis.

S1 Stage fit 35 pts · 21 adjacent 
LET  d = min distance from startup.stage to investor's stages
IF   d = 0 THEN +35  "invests at seed stage"
IF   d = 1 THEN +21  "invests adjacent to seed"

The heaviest signal, because stage is the thing investors are most consistent about publishing and the thing they least often bend on. Adjacent fits keep 60% of the weight — enough to stay visible, not enough to outrank a fund that named your stage.

S2 Geography fit 25 pts · 18 regional 
IF   investor names your market exactly
THEN +25  "covers Malaysia"
ELSE IF investor covers it via a wider region
THEN +18  "covers Malaysia via Southeast Asia"

Naming your country beats covering it regionally. A Kuala Lumpur fund that writes “Malaysia” is a materially better prospect for a Malaysian founder than a global fund that would technically consider you, and the 70% multiplier encodes that.

S3 Sector fit 25 pts · agnostic is neutral 
IF   startup sectors ∩ investor sectors is not empty
THEN +25  "sector match: fintech"
ELSE IF investor is sector-agnostic
THEN +0   "sector-agnostic investor"   // noted, not scored

This is the most consequential design decision in the system. 4,699 of the 6,319 investors here — three quarters — are recorded as sector-agnostic. Penalising them for not declaring a thesis would bury most of the market, so agnostic firms are neutral: they earn no bonus and take no penalty, and the reason line says so plainly.

S4 Cheque-size fit 10 pts 
IF   target raise given AND investor has a ticket range
AND  min <= raise <= max
THEN +10  "cheque range fits US$300K ask"
ELSE      +0   "ask below their US$1M minimum"

Deliberately light, and only applied when you actually state a raise. It is also, right now, inert: no investor in the current knowledge base publishes a per-deal cheque range, so this rule awards nothing to anyone. Fund sizes exist for a handful of firms, but a fund size is not a cheque and Goldo will not estimate one from the other. The rule stays in the engine for when better data arrives — see coverage.

S5 Focus tag 5 pts · tie-breaker 
IF   startup is pre-seed or seed AND tag = "early-stage"
THEN +5   "focuses on early-stage"
IF   startup is later AND tag = "growth-capital"
THEN +5   "focuses on growth capital"

Exists purely to break ties between otherwise identical scores, nudging firms whose declared identity matches where you are in your life as a company.

Limits

Where this method is weakest

Vocabulary is fixed

A sector outside the fifteen labels can't be matched. An unusual startup gets classified into the nearest label or into nothing at all.

Data is as good as its source

Stage and sector come from what firms publish. A fund that quietly changed thesis last quarter still matches its old frame.

No relationship signal

Fit on paper is not warmth. Goldo can't see who has already backed your competitor, or who is out of dry powder.
