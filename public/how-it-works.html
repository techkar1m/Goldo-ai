How it works

From one sentence to a ranked shortlist.

Goldo runs the same six stages on every query. Only the second one involves a model reading your words — everything that decides which investors you see is ordinary, inspectable logic.

Stage 01

Capture what you tell it

You type a description in your own words — no forms, no dropdowns. Something like “we're building a fintech app for gig workers in Kuala Lumpur, pre-seed, raising about US$300K” is plenty. Goldo stores that raw text and works from it.

Stage 02

Turn words into a startup frame

A zero-shot classifier (facebook/bart-large-mnli) reads your description three separate times — once for sector, once for stage, once for geography. Each pass scores your text against a fixed vocabulary that matches the labels used in the investor knowledge base, because a slot that doesn't share vocabulary with the data can never match anything.

Each slot has a confidence floor: 0.55 for sector, 0.45 for stage, 0.50 for geography. Anything below its floor is left empty rather than guessed. This is the only stage where a model touches your query, and it is not allowed to choose investors.

Stage 03

Ask about anything it couldn't read

Empty slots trigger a guided fallback: Goldo asks you directly for the stage, sector or market it couldn't infer, and for your target raise if you haven't mentioned one. If the classifier is cold-starting or rate-limited, the whole conversation falls back to this guided path instead of failing — so the matcher always gets a usable frame.

Stage 04

Eliminate the impossible

Two hard filters run over every investor frame in the knowledge base. An investor is dropped if their stated stages sit more than one rung from yours on the ladder, or if none of their stated regions covers your market. Crucially, an investor is only ever dropped on positive evidence of a mismatch — blank data is not a reason to rule someone out.

Stage 05

Score the survivors

Five weighted rules award points: stage fit is worth up to 35, geography and sector 25 each, cheque-size fit 10, and focus tags 5 as a tie-breaker. Exact hits score full marks and adjacent hits score partial — a seed fund still appears for a pre-seed founder, ranked below the funds that named your stage outright.

Every rule is written out in full on the method page.

Stage 06

Explain the shortlist

You get the top five by score, each with the list of reasons that produced it, how many investors were screened, how many the hard filters ruled out, and a contact route where one is on file. If a contact is flagged as unverified, that flag comes through with it.

Design decisions

Three choices that shape every result

Silence ≠ mismatch

Missing data never eliminates

Most firms in this market publish no sector thesis and no cheque range. Treating silence as a mismatch would bury the majority of the market, so blanks are neutral throughout.

Tolerance = 1

Adjacent stages still show

Funds routinely stretch one rung either side of their stated stage. Goldo keeps neighbours in the running and ranks them below exact matches instead of hiding them.

Rules, not vibes

The model never picks

The classifier only fills in three slots. Choosing and ordering investors is done by rules, so the output can be audited line by line and reproduced.

What you get
  • Up to five investors, ranked by score.
  • The rules that fired for each one, in plain English.
  • How many investors were screened and how many were ruled out.
  • A contact route where one exists, with a verification flag if it needs checking.
What it won't do
  • Introduce you to anyone, or contact investors on your behalf.
  • Predict whether an investor will be interested.
  • Recommend investors outside its knowledge base.
  • Give you financial or fundraising advice.
