How it works
Step 01

Describe your startup

Tell Goldo your sector, funding stage, where you operate, and your target raise — in a sentence, or through quick prompts if you'd rather be guided.

Step 02

The rules screen every investor

Two hard filters drop investors who can't fit your stage or geography. Five weighted scorers rank whoever survives on stage, geography, sector, cheque size and focus.

Step 03

Get a shortlist with reasons

Goldo returns the five best-fit investors and tells you exactly why each one matched — plus a contact route where one is on file.

The method

Not a guess from a language model. A transparent expert system.

Goldo represents each investor as a structured frame and matches with two tiers of production rules. The model only reads your description — the ranking is done by rules you can inspect, so every recommendation is explainable.

Eliminators · hard filters

Stage & geography

An investor is dropped only on positive evidence of a mismatch — a buyout-only fund for a pre-seed startup, or a market they don't cover. Missing data never eliminates anyone.

Scorers · weighted

Stage, geography, sector, cheque, focus

Survivors earn points for exact and adjacent fits. A sector-agnostic or unpriced investor is never penalised for what they simply didn't disclose.

The rules then run over every investor in the knowledge base. Investors whose stated stage sits more than one rung from pre-seed are eliminated, as are those who cover neither Malaysia, Southeast Asia, Asia nor Global. Whoever survives is scored and the top five come back looking like this:

score 76

invests at pre-seed stage; covers Malaysia; sector-agnostic investor; focuses on early-stage
score 65

Every line under a firm name is generated by a rule, not written by a model. If you disagree with a match, you can point at the exact rule that produced it — which is the whole reason Goldo scores with rules instead of asking a model to pick.
